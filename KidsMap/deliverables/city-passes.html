City passes in Canada: coffee and everything else

Nov 8, 2026 · Answers: where the Edmonton CoffeePass came from, what exists in Vancouver, Toronto and other cities, and whether wine, beer, massage and other passes could work for Roam (and Kids Map). Follows the $4.99/month report. [n] = source. ESTIMATE = my own guess.

Short answer
  1. Where CoffeePass came from: I found it while researching the alpha/beta revenue report (Daily Hive article). It's an independent local project in Edmonton, started in Feb 2021 by a local man "to support community", with nearly two dozen cafes 1. The 2026 edition has 40 cafes for $42.75, and it's sold out 2. It's in Edmonton only because that's where its founder lives. Nobody has copied it in Calgary.
  2. Other cities have had passes for 15+ years. Toronto (coffee since 2010, beer since 2014), Ottawa, Montreal, Vancouver (bubble tea, ice cream, breweries), and the BC and Niagara wine regions. Calgary has almost nothing running right now: a 2022 Tourism Calgary beer pass that ended, a Chamber contest, and a cafe guidebook.
  3. It doesn't have to be coffee. The same idea works for yoga and fitness (Passport to Prana runs in Calgary), ice cream and bubble tea (Vancouver), breweries (Toronto, BC) and wine tasting (BC Wine Passport, $85, 103 stops). Each category needs a slightly different setup.
  4. Two problems: ① Alcohol in Alberta: AGLC rules don't allow free drinks or 2-for-1 deals as promotions 14, so a Calgary beer or wine pass can't give free drinks. It has to be a stamp-and-prize pass, or a discount above the minimum price. ② Massage: a free 1-hour massage costs the clinic $100+, so a clinic can only offer "first visit $20 off" or a 15-minute add-on.
  5. My pick: one Roam Pass with a new "edition" each season, not five separate passes. Coffee first (Long Nights, winter). Then ice cream + bubble tea + patios (Long Days, summer). Yoga/fitness first visits all year. Breweries only as a free stamp trail with prizes. Ice cream also works as a Kids Map treat trail.
Correction to my earlier report: I wrote "no Calgary coffee pass". More exactly: no Calgary pass that gives free drinks. There is a Calgary Cafe Passport from The Cafe Passport, an Edmonton company. It's a pocket guide where you rate cafes (the Montreal one is $19), not free coffee 4. Its Calgary page didn't load for me (rate limit), so I couldn't check its price or contents.

1. Where CoffeePass came from, and why Edmonton

☕ CoffeePass (Edmonton)

  • Started: Feb 2021 (CTV: "local man launches CoffeePass to support community"), with nearly two dozen cafes 1.
  • Now (2026 edition): 40 cafes, $42.75, valid to Dec 31, 2026. Printed in limited numbers and sold out 2.
  • How it works: show the card, choose from the CoffeePass menu, and the barista crosses off that cafe's logo. A drip coffee is free; lattes cost a small upgrade fee "to keep costs manageable" for cafes 3. Tip the barista.
  • Who pays the cafe? The FAQ doesn't say. It looks like cafes give the drink free in exchange for a new customer 3.

Why Edmonton and not Calgary?

  • It's a one-person local project, not a company expanding city by city. It grew from about 24 cafes to 40 in 5 years.
  • Edmonton also has The Cafe Passport company (cafe guidebooks for 16 Canadian cities, shipped from Edmonton) 4,5, so Edmonton has a strong indie-cafe community.
  • Calgary had a plan for a coffee pass (PuzzleYYC pilot) that never launched (from our Nov 8 report).
  • What it means for us: the space is open in Calgary. Selling out every year shows demand. Our version would be digital (stamps we already have), so there's no print run and no lost cards.

3. Three kinds of pass (who pays for the free stuff)

A · Paid pass, free item at each stop

The customer pays $15–85 and gets one free item at each place. Places give it free to win a new regular.

Examples: CoffeePass $42.75, BC Wine Passport $85, Passport to Prana $20–35, Niagara $59.62.

Good for: cheap items (coffee, a tasting, a first class). Our money: the pass price.

B · Paid pass, discount at each stop

The customer pays for the right to a discount (a $3 half-pint, BOGO, $20 off). Places still get paid something.

Examples: Toronto Brewery Passport, Craft Beer Passport app, Entertainment® ($4.99/mo).

Good for: expensive items (massage, meals, alcohol in Alberta). Our money: the pass price.

C · Free pass, stamps + prize draw

Free to join. You buy normally, collect stamps, and a full card enters a prize draw. Tourism boards or the places pay for it.

Examples: BC Ale Trail, Vancouver Ice Cream Festival, Tourism Calgary Beers & Cheers.

Good for: alcohol, kids, growth. Our money: sponsors or tourism boards, not users. In Canada a draw needs a skill-testing question.

We already have most of what this needs: stamps, seasons, passport pages and Coins. A pass is just a list of partner places plus a "redeem" button on the stamp flow. All three kinds use the same code.

5. My suggestion: one Roam Pass, a new edition each season

People buy one pass they understand, not five. The "edition" changes with our seasons, so there's always a new reason to buy. Each edition is a list of partner places in the same app.
